\begin{abstract}
The LCN library ships nine marginal-inference engines --- the exact oracle \texttt{ExactInference},
the junction-tree NLP \texttt{CredalJT} (scheme D5), the credal-network family Credal~VE, Credal~CTE,
ApproxLP, Interval~BP, Credal~IJGP, and the structural engines ARIEL and SCC-factor-graph
propagation. Their exactness behavior has been analyzed one engine at a time in six companion notes
(\texttt{ariel\_exactness}, \texttt{cve\_exactness}, \texttt{ccte\_exactness},
\texttt{ibp\_exactness}, \texttt{approxlp\_exactness}, \texttt{tighter\_approximation}). This document
consolidates that analysis into a single reference. We fix one framework --- the LCN distribution set
$\DLCN$, its strong extension $\DSE\supseteq\DLCN$, and the two looseness sources (Gap~A, Gap~B) ---
and then place every engine in it: which feasible set it optimizes, whether its returned interval is
\emph{exact}, a valid \emph{outer} bound, an \emph{inner} bound (hence \emph{not} a valid outer
bound), or \emph{unreliable} (a solver artifact); the precise topological condition under which it is
exact; and its cost. We state the formal results with proof sketches, collected in one place: the
fundamental inclusion $\DLCN\subseteq\DSE$; CVE's exactness over $\DSE$; the exactness of
CVE/IBP/ARIEL on clean singly-connected LCNs; ApproxLP's inner-bound guarantee; IBP's mode dichotomy;
IJGP's exactness at $i$-bound $\ge$ treewidth; ARIEL's structural LMC preservation; and the D5/
CredalJT exactness theorem (hypotheses H1--H4, with the projection/reconstruction proof). Throughout
we separate three distinct kinds of looseness --- \emph{definitional} (an engine that honestly targets
$\DSE$), \emph{solver} (ipopt local optima in \texttt{ExactInference}), and \emph{implementation}
(the now-fixed ARIEL factor-grouping bug; the two now-fixed Interval~BP message bugs) --- and report
the \emph{shipped} reality, not merely the intended theory. A master table and a topology cross-cut table
give the one-screen summary, and a closing decision guide says which engine to reach for.
\end{abstract}

\section{The unifying framework}\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{LCNs, the LCN distribution set, and the marginal task}
An LCN over binary atoms $\mathbf V=\{V_1,\dots,V_n\}$ is a set of probability \emph{sentences}, each
Type-1 $\ell\le P(\varphi)\le u$ or Type-2 $\ell\le P(\varphi\mid\psi)\le u$ over propositional
formulas, together with the conditional independencies of the \emph{Local Markov Condition} (LMC) of
its primal graph. A candidate joint distribution $p\in\Delta(2^{\mathbf V})$ over the $2^n$ truth
assignments belongs to the \emph{LCN distribution set} $\DLCN$ when it meets two families of
requirements:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Sentence bounds.} Each sentence $s$ constrains one probability of $p$ to its interval,
    $\ell_s\le g_s(p)\le u_s$, where $g_s(p)=P(\varphi)$ for a Type-1 sentence and $g_s(p)=P(\varphi\mid
    \psi)$ (a ratio of two marginals of $p$) for a Type-2 sentence.
  \item \emph{LMC equalities.} Each LMC assertion $(X\indep Y\mid S)$ imposes, for \emph{every} joint
    configuration $\mathbf x,\mathbf y,\mathbf s$ of the atom blocks $X,Y,S$, the bilinear equality
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:lmc}
      P(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,\mathbf s)\,P(\mathbf s)=P(\mathbf x,\mathbf s)\,P(\mathbf y,\mathbf s),
    \end{equation}
    i.e.\ $X\indep Y\mid S$ under $p$. We write $h_\iota(p)=0$ for the collection of these equalities
    belonging to assertion $\iota$.
\end{itemize}
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dlcn}
  \DLCN=\bigl\{\,p\in\Delta(2^{\mathbf V})\;:\;\ell_s\le g_s(p)\le u_s\ \text{ for all sentences }s,\quad
  h_\iota(p)=0\ \text{ for all LMC assertions }\iota\,\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The marginal task for a query atom $a$ is to compute its tightest lower and upper probability,
\[
  \lo P(a)=\min_{p\in\DLCN}P(a),\qquad \hi P(a)=\max_{p\in\DLCN}P(a),
\]
and, when evidence $e$ is given, the analogous bounds on the conditional $P(a\mid e)$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Notation (restriction).} For an atom set $A\subseteq\mathbf V$, write
$p\restr A$ for the \emph{marginal of $p$ on $A$} --- the distribution over the truth assignments of
$A$ obtained by summing $p$ over the remaining atoms. Every constraint above is \emph{local}: it
depends on $p$ only through one such marginal --- sentence $s$ through $p\restr\mathrm{atoms}(s)$, and
assertion $(X\indep Y\mid S)$ through $p\restr(X\cup Y\cup S)$. Locality is the lever behind every
exactness result below.

\subsection{The credal network and the strong extension}
When the LCN's structure graph is a chain graph, the joint factorizes over families:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
  P(\mathbf V)=\prod_v P(\mathrm{ch}_v\mid\mathrm{pa}_v),
\end{equation}
and this factorization reproduces exactly the LMC independencies. The \texttt{cn/} pipeline
(\texttt{CredalNetworkVertices.\allowbreak from\_lcn}) compiles the LCN into a \emph{credal network}:
interval-valued local credal sets $K_v$ with enumerated extreme points. The credal-network engines
bound not $\DLCN$
but the \emph{strong extension}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:se}
  \DSE=\Bigl\{\textstyle\prod_v P_v \;:\; P_v\in K_v\text{ chosen independently per parent-config}\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Fundamental inclusion]\label{prop:incl}
$\DLCN\subseteq\DSE$. The inclusion is equality when the primal graph is singly connected and there
is no cross-family sentence (a sentence whose atom scope lies in no single family); it is strict
exactly when a cross-family sentence couples choices that the free product of \eqref{eq:se}
decouples.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Any $p\in\DLCN$ factorizes as \eqref{eq:factor} and each conditional $P(\mathrm{ch}_v\mid\mathrm{pa}_v)$
lies in $K_v$, so $p\in\DSE$: the inclusion holds. A chain-graph product satisfies every LMC
assertion by construction, so a free product in \eqref{eq:se} can leave $\DLCN$ \emph{only} by
violating a \emph{sentence}; when every sentence is intra-family, each $K_v$ already encodes every
sentence constraining its scope and the free product of compliant families is compliant, giving
$\DSE=\DLCN$. A cross-family sentence, by contrast, can be violated by a vertex combination that each
family satisfies locally --- e.g.\ on \texttt{d4\_biting} the free product reaches $P(B\land C)\approx
0.9$ against the sentence bound $0.05$ --- so $\DSE\supsetneq\DLCN$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Consequently any engine that returns a valid bound over $\DSE$ is automatically a valid
\emph{outer} bound over $\DLCN$: $[\lo q_{\mathrm{LCN}},\hi q_{\mathrm{LCN}}]\subseteq[\lo
q_{\mathrm{SE}},\hi q_{\mathrm{SE}}]$. The gap between the two closes precisely under the
Proposition~\ref{prop:incl} condition.

\subsection{The two looseness sources}\label{sec:gaps}
Following \texttt{tighter\_approximation.tex}, the gap between a credal-network engine's output and the
LCN truth has two independent origins.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Gap A (local-set widening).} Each $K_v$ is computed per family in isolation, so it
    admits conditionals no global $p\in\DLCN$ realizes: it ignores sentences on \emph{other} families
    and the cross-family LMC equalities. Formally $K_v^{\mathrm{true}}\subseteq K_v^{\mathrm{local}}$,
    often strictly (e.g.\ the vacuous $[0,1]$ CPT rows on \texttt{alarm}).
  \item \textbf{Gap B (strong-extension widening).} Even with $K_v=K_v^{\mathrm{true}}$, the free
    product of \eqref{eq:se} lets each family pick its vertex independently, violating cross-family
    sentences. This is exactly the strict inclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:incl}.
\end{itemize}
The tightening schemes of \texttt{tighter\_approximation.tex} target these: D1 (\texttt{method="linear-tight"}),
D2 (\texttt{merge\_budget}), D3 (\texttt{solver="scip"}) attack Gap~A at build time and are free for
every credal-network engine; D4 (\texttt{coupling="cross-family"}) and D5 (\texttt{CredalJT}) attack
Gap~B at propagation time.

\subsection{The bound-character taxonomy}\label{sec:taxonomy}
We classify each engine's returned interval $[\lo q,\hi q]$ against the truth $[\lo q^\star,\hi
q^\star]$ (either $\DLCN$ or $\DSE$, stated per engine).
\begin{definition}[Bound character]\label{def:char}
The interval is \emph{exact} if $[\lo q,\hi q]=[\lo q^\star,\hi q^\star]$; a valid \emph{outer} bound
if $[\lo q,\hi q]\supseteq[\lo q^\star,\hi q^\star]$ (sound: it never excludes a feasible marginal);
an \emph{inner} bound if $[\lo q,\hi q]\subseteq[\lo q^\star,\hi q^\star]$ (\emph{not} a valid outer
bound --- it can be too tight); and \emph{unreliable} if neither containment is guaranteed (a solver
or implementation artifact).
\end{definition}
The crucial practical point: an \emph{inner} bound is unsafe as a guarantee --- it may claim more
certainty than the LCN supports. ApproxLP and Interval~BP's variational mode are inner \emph{by
design}; the (now-fixed) ARIEL factor-grouping bug and Interval~BP message bugs used to make those
engines inner \emph{unsoundly}.

\subsection{Three kinds of looseness}\label{sec:threekinds}
It is essential to keep apart \emph{why} an engine's interval is not the LCN truth:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Definitional} --- the engine honestly targets $\DSE$, and $\DSE\supsetneq\DLCN$
    (Gap~B) or $K_v^{\mathrm{local}}\supsetneq K_v^{\mathrm{true}}$ (Gap~A). This is sound looseness:
    the interval is a valid outer bound, just not tight. Closed by D1--D5.
  \item \textbf{Solver} --- the engine targets the right set but uses a local optimizer. This is
    \texttt{ExactInference}'s \texttt{solver="local"} (ipopt on a nonconvex bilinear NLP): a local
    optimum can report a \emph{spuriously inner} (too-tight, unsound) interval. Cured by
    \texttt{solver="global"} (SCIP).
  \item \textbf{Implementation} --- a bug makes the shipped engine depart from its own algorithm.
    Two were on record and \emph{both are now fixed}: the ARIEL factor-grouping bug (which dropped
    the largest factor's sentences, \S\ref{sec:ariel}) and the two Interval~BP message bugs
    (\S\ref{sec:ibp}). We report the \emph{shipped} status, not just the intended theory; with both
    fixes landed the shipped engines now match their algorithms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The exact family: targeting $\DLCN$}\label{sec:exactfamily}

\subsection{\texttt{ExactInference}: the definitional oracle}\label{sec:exact}
\texttt{ExactInference} (\texttt{exact.py}) solves, for each atom, a $\min$ and a $\max$ of $P(a)$ (or
the ratio $P(a\mid e)$) over the full $2^n$ joint subject to \emph{all} sentences and \emph{all} LMC
equalities --- i.e.\ directly over $\DLCN$ of \eqref{eq:dlcn}. It is the definitional target, not a
member of the credal-network family; in particular it does \emph{not} bound $\DSE$. Two backends,
selected by the \texttt{solver} argument of \texttt{run}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{solver="global"}: SCIP spatial branch-and-bound. Certifies the global optimum, or on
    time-out returns the best bound with its optimality gap. \texttt{self.status} records the per-atom
    verdict (\texttt{confirmed} / \texttt{unconfirmed} / \texttt{unsolved}).
  \item \texttt{solver="local"} (default): ipopt with a two-phase SLSQP fallback
    (\texttt{find\_feasible\_points} $\to$ \texttt{optimize\_\allowbreak marginal\_slsqp}) and
    multi-restart. Fast, but a \emph{local} method on a nonconvex bilinear-LMC NLP.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Global certified; local unreliable]\label{prop:exactinf}
With \texttt{solver="global"} and reported gap $\le$ \texttt{gap\_tol}, \texttt{ExactInference}
returns the exact $\DLCN$ bounds. With \texttt{solver="local"}, the returned interval is
\emph{unreliable} (Definition~\ref{def:char}): it may be spuriously inner (too tight), because a
local optimum of the nonconvex NLP can sit strictly inside the true range.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The model \eqref{eq:dlcn} is exactly $\DLCN$, so any \emph{globally} optimal $\min/\max$ equals the
true bound; SCIP's spatial B\&B certifies global optimality up to the gap tolerance. ipopt, being a
local NLP solver on a nonconvex feasible region (the bilinear LMC equalities are nonconvex), can
converge to a stationary point whose objective is strictly interior to the true extremum; the
multi-restart + SLSQP fallback mitigates but does not certify. Hence the local backend carries no
soundness guarantee.
\end{proof}

\noindent \emph{Witness (shipped).} On \texttt{alarm.lcn} the certified bound is $P(A)=[0.087,0.100]$
(brute-force / SCIP), whereas raw \texttt{solver="local"} reports the spuriously tighter
$P(A)=[0.092,0.100]$, $P(E)=[0.050,0.073]$ --- an \emph{inner}, unsound interval. This is the reason
the companion notes treat \texttt{ExactInference}-local as \emph{not} a reliable oracle and prefer
SCIP-backed cross-checks (\texttt{verify\_scip.py}, \texttt{verify\_bruteforce.py}); \texttt{CredalJT}
is often the more trustworthy engine on such instances (\S\ref{sec:d5}).

\subsection{\texttt{CredalJT} (D5): exact at treewidth cost}\label{sec:d5}
\texttt{CredalJT} (\texttt{cn/junction\_nlp.py}) builds one junction (clique) tree from the chain-graph
elimination order, gives each cluster $C$ a variable vector $q_C$ over the joint of its atoms, ties
adjacent clusters by Lauritzen separator-consistency equalities, and hosts every sentence and LMC
equality on a cluster containing its atoms. It reads each query marginal off a host cluster and
optimizes $\min/\max$ with SCIP. Cost is $\sum_C 2^{|\mathrm{atoms}(C)|}$ --- exponential in
\emph{treewidth}, not in $n$.

We compare two feasible sets. The \emph{joint-feasible set} is $\mathcal F:=\DLCN$ of
\eqref{eq:dlcn}: full joint distributions $p$ over all $2^n$ assignments meeting every sentence and
LMC constraint. The \emph{cluster-feasible set} $\mathcal G$ replaces the one global joint by one
local distribution $q_C\in\Delta(2^{|\mathrm{atoms}(C)|})$ per cluster $C$ of the junction tree $T$,
required only to be locally consistent and to satisfy the constraints each cluster hosts:
\begin{align*}
  \mathcal G=\bigl\{(q_C)_{C\in T}:\ &\textstyle\sum_i q_C[i]=1\ \text{ for each cluster }C
    \quad(\text{each }q_C\text{ is a distribution});\\
  &q_C\restr S=q_D\restr S\ \text{ on every tree edge }(C,D),\ S{=}C\cap D
    \quad(\text{clusters agree on their separator});\\
  &\text{every sentence / LMC equality hosted on a cluster holds there}\bigr\},
\end{align*}
where $q_C\restr S$ is the marginal of the cluster distribution $q_C$ onto the separator atoms $S$
(the ``$M_{C\to S}q_C$'' of the implementation --- the linear map that sums $q_C$ down to $S$). An
LMC assertion $(X\indep Y\mid S)$ is \emph{hosted} if some cluster contains $X\cup Y\cup S$ (so its
equality \eqref{eq:lmc} can be written on that cluster), and \emph{separator-entailed} if $S$
separates $X$ from $Y$ in the chordal graph $G^\ast$ whose maximal cliques are the clusters.

\medskip\noindent\emph{The read-off functional.} Fix a query atom $a$ and evidence $e$, and let $C_a$
be a cluster containing $a$ together with the evidence atoms (H4 below guarantees one exists). Define
the \emph{read-off} $\readoff_a$, the marginal that \texttt{CredalJT} reports for $a$, directly from
the host cluster distribution:
\[
  \readoff_a(q)=\!\!\sum_{i\,:\,a=1\text{ in state }i}\!\! q_{C_a}[i]
  \quad(\text{no evidence}),\qquad
  \readoff_a(q)=\frac{\sum_{i\,:\,a=1,\;e\text{ in state }i} q_{C_a}[i]}
                     {\sum_{i\,:\,e\text{ in state }i} q_{C_a}[i]}
  \quad(\text{with evidence }e).
\]
Write $\pi(p)=(p\restr C)_{C\in T}$ for the \emph{projection} of a joint $p$ onto the clusters. The
same formula applied to $\pi(p)$ returns exactly $p$'s own marginal, $\readoff_a(\pi(p))=P_p(a)$
(resp.\ $P_p(a\mid e)$); so the theorem below compares \emph{one} functional $\readoff_a$ optimized
over $\mathcal G$ against the same functional optimized over $\mathcal F$.

\begin{theorem}[D5 exactness]\label{thm:d5}
Suppose \textup{(H1)} $T$ has the running-intersection property (RIP; always true by construction);
\textup{(H2)} every LCN sentence is hosted; \textup{(H3)} every LMC assertion is either hosted or
separator-entailed in $G^\ast$; \textup{(H4)} the query atom and all evidence atoms lie together in
some cluster. Then for every such atom $a$ the read-off optimized over the cluster-feasible set
equals the true marginal bound optimized over the joint-feasible set,
\[
  \min_{q\in\mathcal G}\readoff_a(q)=\min_{p\in\mathcal F}\readoff_a(\pi(p))=\lo P(a),
  \qquad
  \max_{q\in\mathcal G}\readoff_a(q)=\max_{p\in\mathcal F}\readoff_a(\pi(p))=\hi P(a),
\]
and likewise for a conditional query $P(a\mid e)$. That is, \texttt{CredalJT} returns exactly the
\texttt{ExactInference} bounds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\emph{Projection $\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ (always valid; needs only H1/H4).} Given a joint
$p\in\mathcal F$, set each cluster distribution to the corresponding marginal, $q_C:=p\restr C$. Then
every $q_C$ is a distribution; each separator equality holds because both $q_C\restr S$ and
$q_D\restr S$ equal $p\restr S$; and every hosted constraint holds by locality (its value on $q_C$
equals its value on $p$). Here $q=\pi(p)$, so the read-off identity gives
$\readoff_a(q)=\readoff_a(\pi(p))=P_p(a)$.
Hence $q\in\mathcal G$ with the same objective value, so $\min_{\mathcal G}\readoff_a\le
\min_{\mathcal F}\readoff_a$ and $\max_{\mathcal G}\readoff_a\ge\max_{\mathcal F}\readoff_a$: the
cluster interval \emph{contains} the joint interval, i.e.\ \textbf{CredalJT is always a valid outer
bound.}

\emph{Reconstruction $\mathcal G\to\mathcal F$ (needs H2/H3).} Conversely, given a separator-consistent
family $(q_C)\in\mathcal G$, the RIP marginal-consistency theorem (Lauritzen) yields a \emph{unique}
joint $p=\prod_C q_C\big/\prod_{(C,D)\in T}q_{S}$ (with $q_S$ the common separator marginal) whose
cluster marginals are exactly the $q_C$. We check $p\in\mathcal F$. Each \emph{hosted} sentence or LMC
equality holds because, by locality, its value on $p$ equals its value on the host cluster's marginal
$q_{C_h}$, which satisfies it (H2 and the hosted part of H3). Each \emph{separator-entailed} LMC
$(X\indep Y\mid S)$ holds \emph{automatically}: since $p$ factorizes over $T$ it obeys the global
Markov property of the chordal graph $G^\ast$, so whenever $S$ separates $X$ from $Y$ in $G^\ast$, $X$
and $Y$ are conditionally independent given $S$ \emph{under this reconstructed $p$} --- even though
the equality was never imposed on $\mathcal G$ (the other part of H3). Thus each cluster family lifts
to a joint of the same objective value, giving the reverse inclusion. Both inclusions force equality;
by H4 the conditional read-off $\readoff_a$ is a functional of the single host cluster $C_a$, so the
same two maps preserve it.
\end{proof}

\noindent Three consequences. (a) The proof never mentions chains: exactness is a property of the
\emph{junction tree}, not of chains. (b) The projection half is unconditional, so \texttt{CredalJT} is
\emph{never unsound} --- its interval always contains the truth; H2/H3 decide only tightness. (c) On a
Markov chain the moral graph is already triangulated and every LMC is single-separator, so H3 holds
with treewidth $1$. \emph{H3 is sufficient but not necessary}: on \texttt{asia} and \texttt{polytree}
some LMCs are neither hosted nor separator-entailed yet CredalJT is empirically exact (the unentailed
assertions do not bind); on \texttt{smokers} the six large-scope LMCs of the undirected clique
$F_1F_2F_3$ are unhostable and unentailed, H3 fails \emph{and bites}, and CredalJT returns the loose
but valid $P(F_1)=[0,1]$.

\section{The credal-network family: targeting $\DSE$}\label{sec:cnfamily}

All five engines here consume the same \texttt{CredalNetworkVertices}; they differ only in how they
propagate the enumerated extreme points, and hence in where they land relative to $[\lo
q_{\mathrm{SE}},\hi q_{\mathrm{SE}}]$.

\subsection{Credal~VE: exact over the strong extension}\label{sec:cve}
Credal~VE (\texttt{cve.py}) computes every atom's bounds by looping a single-query bucket elimination,
each time recomputing an elimination order in which the target is eliminated \emph{last}.

\begin{proposition}[CVE exact over $\DSE$]\label{prop:cve}
With \texttt{coupling="off"} and exact pruning ($\epsilon{=}0$), Credal~VE returns exactly $[\lo
q_{\mathrm{SE}}(a),\hi q_{\mathrm{SE}}(a)]$ for every atom, hence a valid outer bound on $\DLCN$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Every potential CVE forms is a finite set of products of local extreme points, i.e.\ a set of $\DSE$
vertices; combine and marginalize preserve this, and pruning removes only dominated functions, which
never attain a componentwise extremum. Because $\DSE$ is a product of finitely many polytopes, each
bound is attained at a vertex combination; eliminating the query \emph{last} keeps that combination
in the surviving potential through to the read-off, so the reported $\min/\max$ equals the $\DSE$
extremum. See \texttt{cve\_exactness.tex} \S3.
\end{proof}

\noindent Combined with Proposition~\ref{prop:incl}, Credal~VE is \emph{exact w.r.t.\ $\DLCN$} exactly
when $\DSE=\DLCN$ (singly connected, no cross-family sentence). It is the ``gold'' member of the
family: exact over $\DSE$, whereas the others are inner (ApproxLP), outer with loop slack (IBP loopy),
or order-sensitive (CTE).

\subsection{Credal~CTE: exact over $\DSE$, but order-sensitive}\label{sec:cte}
Credal~CTE (\texttt{ccte.py}) is the two-pass (collect + distribute) bucket-tree engine: one shared
elimination order produces all marginals at once. With $\epsilon{=}0$ it is exact over $\DSE$ on a
clean singly-connected sentence-free LCN (the shared schedule is a genuine clique tree there). But its
single shared order --- deliberately \emph{not} augmented per query, unlike Credal~VE --- can prune the
function carrying an extremal marginal at an intermediate scope where it is dominated. The witness is
\texttt{d4\_biting}: the true $\DSE$-and-$\DLCN$ value is $P(B)=[0.04,0.72]$ (Credal~VE, CredalJT, and
two oracles agree), yet Credal~CTE reports the strictly \emph{inner} $[0.05,0.65]$ (and CTE${+}$D4
$[0.05,0.55]$). So on cross-family instances CTE can fall inside $[\lo q_{\mathrm{SE}},\hi
q_{\mathrm{SE}}]$ --- \emph{not even a valid outer bound}. See \texttt{ccte\_exactness.tex}.

\subsection{ApproxLP: an inner bound}\label{sec:approxlp}
ApproxLP (\texttt{approxlp.py}) reformulates the marginal problem as a multilinear program and solves
it by coordinate descent: fix all families but one, pick that family's best extreme point (a linear
subproblem), sweep to convergence.

\begin{proposition}[ApproxLP inner]\label{prop:approxlp}
With \texttt{coupling="off"}, ApproxLP returns an interval contained in $[\lo q_{\mathrm{SE}},\hi
q_{\mathrm{SE}}]$; in general it is \emph{not} a valid outer bound on $\DLCN$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Every state ApproxLP evaluates is a single feasible product joint (each family at one extreme point,
or the center), i.e.\ a point of $\DSE$, and the objective is evaluated exactly on that precise Bayes
net. The reported $\min$/$\max$ is therefore a $\min$/$\max$ over a \emph{subset} of $\DSE$ (the
coordinate-descent trajectory), hence no smaller / no larger than the global $\DSE$ extremum:
$\lo q_{\mathrm{SE}}\le\lo q_{\mathrm{ALP}}\le\hi q_{\mathrm{ALP}}\le\hi q_{\mathrm{SE}}$. See
\texttt{approxlp\_exactness.tex} \S2.
\end{proof}

\noindent The inner gap is strict only when the nonconvex program makes descent stall at a non-global
local optimum. On the instances tested (\texttt{d4\_biting}, 2-atom chain) it is \emph{sound and
tight}: it reproduces the exact $P(B)=[0.04,0.72]$. Its natural use is the \emph{inner} half of a
two-sided bracket: ApproxLP (inner) with Credal~VE (exact over $\DSE$) on the same vertices sandwich
the truth and detect stalls.

\subsection{Interval~BP: outer / inner by mode; exact on trees}\label{sec:ibp}
Interval~BP (\texttt{ibp.py}, class \texttt{IntervalBP}) is loopy interval message passing over the
factor graph. \texttt{method="interval"} passes per-state intervals and is an \emph{outer}
approximation of $[\lo q_{\mathrm{SE}},\hi q_{\mathrm{SE}}]$; \texttt{method="variational"} runs
mean-field over a finite sample of vertex combinations and is an \emph{inner} approximation.

\begin{proposition}[IBP interval outer; exact on tree]\label{prop:ibp}
With \texttt{method="interval"}, Interval~BP returns a valid outer bound over $\DSE$. When the factor
graph is a tree (Markov chain, tree, polytree) it is exact over $\DSE$; combined with
Proposition~\ref{prop:incl} it is then exact over $\DLCN$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each factor$\to$child update ranges over all local extreme points and all corner \emph{distributions}
of the incoming messages, so every $\DSE$ marginal is contained in the message interval; a conditional
factor sends the vacuous $[0,1]$ upward, excluding no parent marginal; variable$\to$factor messages
only intersect. On a tree there is a unique path between factors, so no message feeds back and no
correlated pair's corners over-cover, giving the exact $\DSE$ range. See \texttt{ibp\_exactness.tex}.
\end{proof}

\noindent \emph{Shipped status: fixed.} Two message bugs previously made the interval mode inner and
unsound --- a spurious normalized-likelihood upward message (collapsing an unconstrained root to
$[0.5,0.5]$) and an invalid component-wise corner enumeration. Both are now corrected; the shipped
engine reproduces Credal~VE on the 2-atom chain ($P(x_0)=[0.30,0.50]$, was $[0.5,0.5]$) and on
\texttt{d4\_biting} ($P(B)=[0.04,0.72]$, was $[0.05,0.65]$). One residual limitation is architectural:
the intersection message algebra performs no Bayesian \emph{upward} update, so evidence on a child
does not shift a parent's posterior --- for conditional queries with evidence on descendants use
Credal~VE / \texttt{CredalJT}.

\subsection{Credal~IJGP: exact at $i$-bound $\ge$ treewidth}\label{sec:ijgp}
Credal~IJGP (\texttt{ijgp.py}) runs iterative join-graph propagation with a mini-bucket
\texttt{i\_bound} capping cluster size, forward+backward passes to a message-change threshold.

\begin{proposition}[IJGP exact at large $i$-bound]\label{prop:ijgp}
When \texttt{i\_bound} $\ge$ the treewidth of the credal network, the join graph is a genuine junction
tree and Credal~IJGP is exact over $\DSE$; with $\DSE=\DLCN$ it is exact over $\DLCN$. For smaller
\texttt{i\_bound} it is an outer-style approximation, tunable by the $i$-bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At \texttt{i\_bound} $\ge$ treewidth no mini-bucket partitioning is forced, so the join graph carries
no cycle and message passing is exact elimination over the enumerated vertices (the same argument as
Interval~BP on a tree, one level up at cluster granularity). Below treewidth, mini-bucket splitting
introduces the loopy slack that the $i$-bound trades against cost $2^{i}$/cluster.
\end{proof}

\section{Structural and hybrid engines: targeting $\DLCN$ by decomposition}\label{sec:structural}

\subsection{ARIEL: structural LMC preservation}\label{sec:ariel}
ARIEL (\texttt{ariel.py}) runs interval message passing on the bipartite factor graph whose factor
nodes group sentences by shared atom scope.

\begin{proposition}[ARIEL exact on singly-connected]\label{prop:ariel}
On a singly-connected LCN, ARIEL returns the exact $\DLCN$ marginal bounds. For every LMC assertion
$(X\indep Y\mid S)$: if $X\cup Y\cup S$ fits in one factor, the bilinear equality is imposed in that
factor's local NLP; otherwise the assertion factorizes into single-separator independencies along the
unique factor path, each realized exactly by an interval message across that separator. The interval
summary is lossless precisely because a tree has no second path along which a discarded joint
correlation could matter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Structural LMC preservation on a tree factor graph (\texttt{ariel\_exactness.tex} Prop.~1). On the
shipped chain/tree/polytree, \emph{no} LMC fits inside a single factor, so none is imposed as an
explicit constraint, yet the assertions survive structurally along the tree paths, giving exactness.
On a loopy factor graph the interval message across a boundary variable loses the joint correlation
that a second path needs, so ARIEL is a valid outer bound but not tight (the coupled-diamond example,
$P(D)=[0.316,0.684]$ vs exact $[0.316,0.594]$).
\end{proof}

\noindent \emph{Shipped status: fixed.} An earlier factor-graph construction
(\texttt{factor\_graph.py}, \texttt{FactorGraph.\allowbreak\_make\_factor\_nodes}) had an off-by-one
labelling bug: it created the first factor before the grouping loop and then reused the label
\texttt{f1} for the next new-scope factor, silently overwriting --- and dropping the sentences of ---
the first, largest-scope factor (sentences are sorted by decreasing scope). The \emph{shipped} ARIEL
therefore returned vacuous $[0,1]$ interior marginals on chain/tree/polytree, contradicting
Proposition~\ref{prop:ariel}. The bug is now fixed (each new-scope factor gets a fresh
post-incremented label). Verified: on the two-atom chain the fixed engine returns
$P(x_0)=[0.30,0.50]$, $P(x_1)=[0.40,0.68]$, matching Credal~VE / \texttt{ExactInference}; all tests
pass. See \texttt{ariel\_exactness.tex}.

\subsection{SCCP / SCCP-ARIEL: exactness on the acyclic condensation}\label{sec:sccp}
The SCC-factor-graph engines (\texttt{sccp.py}, \texttt{sccp\_ariel.py}) contract each strongly
connected component of the structure graph into a super-node, giving an acyclic condensation DAG, and
run two-pass (collect + distribute) belief propagation on it. On the condensation the propagation is
exact elimination; the only inexactness is \emph{inside} a cyclic super-node, whose local nonconvex
NLP is solved by ipopt (\texttt{sccp.py}, local $\Rightarrow$ the same reliability caveat as
\texttt{ExactInference}-local) or approximately by ARIEL loopy BP (\texttt{sccp\_ariel.py}). Hence:
when every SCC is trivial (a singleton, whose local problem is an LP), the two-pass propagation is
exact over $\DLCN$; a non-trivial SCC introduces solver looseness (v1) or approximation (v2). See
\texttt{scc\_factor\_graph.tex}.

\section{Reliability and verification}\label{sec:reliability}

Three verifiers cross-check the engines against ground truth (\texttt{lcn/inference/marginal/}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{verify\_scip.py} --- certified global (SCIP spatial B\&B) marginal bounds; the
    reference oracle when it terminates within the gap tolerance.
  \item \texttt{verify\_bruteforce.py} --- LMC-aware brute-force enumeration cross-check; found the
    \texttt{ExactInference}-local over-tightening on \texttt{alarm}.
  \item \texttt{verify\_lmc\_factorization.py} --- solver-free check that the credal network's product
    joints satisfy the LMC (certifies $\DSE=\DLCN$ on chain/tree/polytree, i.e.\ Gap~B$=0$).
\end{itemize}
The reliability lessons carried into Table~\ref{tab:master}: \texttt{ExactInference}-local is a
\emph{local} solver (use \texttt{solver="global"} when a guarantee is needed); the ARIEL
factor-grouping bug and the two Interval~BP message bugs are all \emph{fixed}, so those shipped
engines now match their algorithms; Credal~CTE is order-sensitive and can be inner on cross-family
instances. When two
engines that both claim ``exact over $\DSE$'' (CVE and CTE) disagree, the disagreement localizes an
order-sensitivity or a bug, not a modeling difference.

\section{Decision guide}\label{sec:guide}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Need a certified guarantee, small $n$:} \texttt{ExactInference} with
    \texttt{solver="global"}. Never trust \texttt{solver="local"} as a guarantee.
  \item \textbf{Need exactness at scale, bounded treewidth:} \texttt{CredalJT} (D5) --- exact under
    H1--H4 at cost $\sum_C 2^{|C|}$, always a valid outer bound otherwise. This is the recommended
    exact engine for chain-graph LCNs, and is often more trustworthy than
    \texttt{ExactInference}-local (\texttt{alarm}).
  \item \textbf{All singleton marginals over $\DSE$, exactly:} Credal~VE. Prefer over Credal~CTE when
    a cross-family sentence is present (CTE can fall inner).
  \item \textbf{A two-sided sandwich of the truth:} pair Credal~VE (outer over $\DSE$) with ApproxLP
    (inner over $\DSE$) on the same vertices; equal endpoints certify the $\DSE$ bound.
  \item \textbf{Fast, singly-connected:} Interval~BP \texttt{method="interval"} or ARIEL --- both
    exact on trees now that their respective bugs are fixed.
  \item \textbf{Tighten any $\DSE$-family engine toward $\DLCN$:} apply the build-stage schemes
    D1 (\texttt{method=\allowbreak"linear-tight"}), D2 (\texttt{merge\_budget}),
    D3 (\texttt{solver="scip"}) first --- free for all of them --- then D4/D5 for cross-family
    coupling.
\end{itemize}
